About Enzz

Why "ecosystem" isn't just a name.

Most tools built for business, learning and work are designed for people who already have access. We design ours for people who don't yet.

"We build technology to increase equality — so a shop owner in an informal market, a learner in a rural school, and a job seeker without formal certification all get the same shot as anyone else."

Our apps are built to work as one connected system: a business on Biashara is discoverable on Pata, a learner on Taalo is a future candidate on AOIS. Nobody has to start from zero twice.

The central idea is simple — technology should close gaps, not widen them. Every Enzz feature is judged first by what access problem it solves, and second by how convenient it feels.

The problem

The digital divide has changed shape.

The question used to be "does someone have a smartphone?" Kenya has largely answered that. The real question now is whether people can turn connectivity into economic opportunity — and that's the gap Enzz targets.

Why Kenya

A strong starting market, for five concrete reasons.

01

Mobile-first consumers

Mobile connectivity is deeply embedded in everyday economic activity, and smartphone adoption keeps rising.

02

A huge MSME economy

The World Bank cites roughly 7.4 million MSMEs in Kenya, with 94% unlicensed being micro-to-mid level businesses with no concrete online presence—exactly who Biashara is designed for.

03

Informal businesses drive employment

Kenya's 2025 Economic Survey reported that most new jobs created in 2024 came from the informal sector, predominantly MSMEs — making small-business digitalization an economic infrastructure opportunity, not just a software one.

04

Education is undergoing structural change

Kenya continues implementing CBC/CBE, with KICD providing curriculum designs. CBC asks whether a learner can reliably do something unaided, in more than one setting — a question most digitized marks-and-averages tools can't actually answer. That gap is what Taalo is built to close.

05

Workforce technology is increasingly important

Kenya's employment ecosystem is actively moving toward more inclusive digital employment services, with public systems modernizing to match.

How it fits together

Not four apps. One opportunity loop.

A learner develops competencies through Taalo. Those competencies become a verified skills profile on AOIS. A worker earns income and starts a business through Biashara. That business becomes discoverable on Pata — which sends customers, and demand, back into the ecosystem.

How we build

Three principles behind every feature.

AI at the core

Every app uses AI to reduce manual work and surface insight — inventory forecasting, business comparison, and in Taalo, reading a learner's true mastery from their evidence trail instead of a score average.

Built for local reality

Designed around low-cost smartphones and the real constraints of Kenyan businesses, schools and job seekers — not adapted from elsewhere.

Connected by design

Our apps share a common backbone, so progress in one part of a person's life — business, learning, work — lifts the rest.
